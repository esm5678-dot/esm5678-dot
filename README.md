# Lee Seung Min

**AI Product Builder · Developer**  
Building practical AI workflows with clear human control.

## About Me

I explore how AI can make everyday services more useful, from phone assistance to creative tools and small-business workflows. My work combines product prototyping, API integration, safety boundaries, and testing. I use AI-assisted development and collaborate with teammates to turn ideas into working experiments.

## Featured Projects

### CELLEN — AI-assisted phone service
An in-development phone assistance project exploring how AI can handle conversations while the user retains the final decision. The prototype combines a Node.js backend, telephony and realtime AI integrations, with approval and handoff boundaries. Simulations and real calls are different evidence; production readiness remains subject to verification. [Project notes](projects/cellen.md)

### NOVELFORGE — AI-assisted writing studio
An experimental workflow for drafting, editing, reviewing and versioning long-form fiction. It explores provider integrations and author approval before changes are applied. A private beta safety branch remains under review; operational recovery and alerting still need verification. [Project notes](projects/novelforge.md)

### PROJECT ZERO HUMAN — an AI-led business experiment
An experiment in building a small-business inquiry workflow with no additional cash spending. Its offline ZERO OPS prototype uses deterministic classification and synthetic data; it is not a live CRM or an AI model. [Public repository](https://github.com/esm5678-dot/Project-zero-human-) · [Project notes](projects/zero-human.md)

## AI & LLM Architecture

I work on connecting model APIs to product workflows, separating simulated tests from real provider calls, and designing approval points around consequential actions. In CELLEN, the user’s final decision is a central product principle. In NOVELFORGE, proposed edits are reviewed before a new manuscript version is saved.

## Engineering Workflow

Define the problem → prototype → test with scoped evidence → document limitations → review before release. I use branches and pull requests to keep work reviewable, and distinguish local validation from operational readiness.
